[ BAWAF ]

Scientific Name:

BAWAF

Discovered by / at:

Dr. Nailong, 6:07 AM, on a lab server that refused to shut down.

Family:

Codeater. A family of parasitic organisms that live only inside running machines. BAWAF is the only member that has been seen moving on a screen.

Habitat:

The black space behind the screen, where memory is full of code that nobody uses. It prefers machines that are left on overnight.

Appearance:

A few tiny red cells that never sit still. Up close, a BAWAF looks like a cluster of trembling red dots. Everywhere it goes, it leaves a red trail that does not fade, a record of the code it has already eaten.

Key Characteristics:

  • Feeds on code.
  • Leaves a permanent red trail.
  • Splits rapidly when clicked.
  • Drawn to the cursor.

What we know so far about BAWAF? BAWAF was first noticed as a strange red stain on a black diagnostic screen. At first the engineers thought it was a broken pixel, until it moved. Since then, researchers have learned that BAWAF lives inside the computer itself and survives by eating code. The flickering numbers in the background are its food. Every step it takes, it eats a little more, and the red trail it leaves behind is all that remains of that code. Restarting the computer does not kill BAWAF. It is not aggressive, but it is curious. It seems to recognize the cursor as another living thing and will crowd toward it when called. Some technicians say they can hear it: a low hum while it rests, and a different sound when it starts to chase.
Guidelines for human interaction with this creature Left-click anywhere on the screen to draw a BAWAF up from the deeper layers of code to the surface, where it can be seen. Hold the right mouse button near a BAWAF to call it. Nearby BAWAFs will follow your cursor, and you will hear them start to explore. Let go of the right button, or move too far away, and they will calm down and wander again. Their trails are permanent: the longer you watch, the more of the screen they eat. Turn your sound on. It makes interesting sounds.
